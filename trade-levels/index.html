Trade Levels

See when a stock is usually cheap (a time to buy), when it is usually expensive (a time to sell), and which prices to use. Made for beginners.

Data and settings

Sign up free at twelvedata.com (no card, 800 requests a day), paste the key once, then tap Get prices. It loads the longest daily history your key allows (up to about 120 years, usually 10 to 30) and about 60 days of 5-minute prices. Prices on the free plan can be delayed up to 15 minutes. This works when the page is opened in Chrome or Safari. Claude's built-in preview blocks it; use the file buttons below there.

US stocks trade 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM New York time. Change "Day starts" and "Day ends" to include earlier or later trading.

No key? Get 5-minute prices with Python

This writes a file the "Load 5-min CSV" button can read, already in New York time. I could not run it from here, so if it errors, send me the message.

pip install yfinance

import yfinance as yf
df = yf.download("AA", period="1mo", interval="5m", auto_adjust=False)
df.columns = df.columns.get_level_values(0)
df.index = df.index.tz_convert("America/New_York").tz_localize(None)
df.index.name = "datetime"
df[["Open","High","Low","Close","Volume"]].to_csv("aa_5m.csv")

Look back over

Low is the cheapest price of a day, a good time to buy. High is the most expensive price of a day, a good time to sell. This shows when those usually happened.

How long does this take?

The free market key allows 5 requests a minute and the free Twelve Data key 8 a minute. Step 1 downloads one request per trading day (1 day: about 15 seconds, 1 week: about 1 minute, 1 month: about 5 minutes). Step 2 downloads one request per stock, about 7 minutes for 50. Keep the page open and the screen on. Results are saved in this browser, so the next visit is instant. Step 2 uses 50 of your 800 daily Twelve Data requests.

Momentum list (reel rules)

Finds stocks priced $1 to $20, up 10% or more on the last day, with volume 5 times or more its usual level. It uses the data from step 1 above, so it adds no extra requests.

How to use this page

  1. Type a stock ticker (the short code, like AA or NVDA) and tap Get prices. Until you load real prices, every number is made-up example data.
  2. Open Summary. It says whether the price setup looks ready, and the time of day when prices are usually lowest (buy) and highest (sell).
  3. Open Times to see that pattern for 1 day, 3 days, 1 week or 1 month, slot by slot, and day by day.
  4. Open Prices for exact dollar levels: where to buy, where to cut a loss (stop-loss), and where to sell for a profit.
  5. Never put in money you can't afford to lose. Start by watching, or use a practice (paper trading) account.

Words you will see

Ticker
The short code for a company's stock, like NVDA for Nvidia.
Share
One small piece of ownership in a company. The price shown is the price of one share.
High and low
The highest and lowest price the stock reached in a time period. Buying low and selling high is how traders make money.
Buy zone / Sell zone
The times of day when, in the days checked, the low (buy zone) or the high (sell zone) happened most often.
Stop-loss
A price you decide in advance. If the stock falls to it, you sell and accept a small loss so it doesn't become a big one.
Take profit / sell target
A price above where you bought, where you plan to sell and keep the gain.
R (reward and risk)
1R is the amount you lose if your stop-loss is hit. A 2R target means a gain twice that size. Aim for plans where the possible gain is bigger than the possible loss.
Uptrend / downtrend
Uptrend: price is climbing over weeks, so dips are often bought. Downtrend: price is falling, so buying is riskier.
Moving average (20-day, 50-day)
The average closing price of the last 20 or 50 days. It smooths the wiggles so you can see direction. Price above the 50-day average is a sign of strength.
Breakout
Price pushes above its recent high, which can mean buyers are taking over.
Pullback
A temporary dip inside an uptrend, often used as a cheaper place to buy.
ATR
Average True Range: how many dollars the stock normally moves in a day. Stops are set using it so normal wiggles don't knock you out.
RSI
A 0 to 100 score of recent buying pressure. Over 70 means it rose a lot lately (can be stretched). Under 30 means it fell a lot lately.
Position size
How many shares to buy so that if your stop-loss is hit, you lose only the amount you chose, like 1% of your account.
Hindsight
Looking back, the best buy and sell are obvious. In real time you don't know the day's low or high until the day ends.
Pre-market and after-hours
Trading outside 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM. Fewer people trade then, prices jump more, and some brokers limit it.

Common questions

Why can't I just buy at the lowest time every day?

The low is only known after the day is over. This page shows when lows tend to happen, which only nudges the odds. On many days the pattern does not hold, and news can break any pattern.

Does a buy time and a sell time on the same day mean a day trade?

Yes. Buying and selling the same stock on the same day is a day trade. In the US, a margin account with under $25,000 is limited to 3 day trades in 5 days (the pattern day trader rule). Check with your broker; rules change.

How accurate are these times?

They are counts from past days. A pattern seen on 5 days can be luck. Look for a time slot that shows up on well over a third of 20 or more days, and treat it as a hint, never a promise.

Where does the Top 50 list come from?

From real end-of-day prices for every US stock, downloaded from Massive (formerly Polygon.io). Each stock is scored on how much it moves in a day and how many dollars trade, because day traders need both. Tiny, thinly traded stocks are left out. It is a ranking of what was active, not a list of stocks to buy.

What if the stock drops after I buy?

That is what the stop-loss is for. Decide it before you buy, and stick to it. Without a stop, a small loss can turn into a large one.
